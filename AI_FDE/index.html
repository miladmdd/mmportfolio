Case study · Forward deployed

Ordering at the drive-through, by hand

From a noisy speaker box to a quiet, accessible ordering channel running on one edge device in the lane.

The problem

The speaker box is the weakest link in the drive-through

Noise

Engines, wind and traffic drown out the customer, so staff have to ask again.

Accuracy

Misheard items turn into remakes, refunds and a slower lane for everyone behind.

Access

Customers who are hard of hearing or speech-impaired are left out.

Language

Accents and non-native speakers are easily misunderstood over a crackly speaker.

For this client: their drive-through sits in a noisy, busy area, and orders were regularly coming through wrong.

The solution

Try the gesture menu

A camera at window height tracks the driver's hand. Each gesture maps to an ordering action, and the screen confirms every step. Voice ordering stays available: gestures are an extra, quieter channel. Use the gestures below to order.

    Engineering

    Key technical decisions

    AreaChoiceWhy
    Hand trackingMediaPipe HandsFast, proven landmarks; no face data needed
    Gesture modelTwo parts: a custom MediaPipe Gesture Recognizer for poses, plus landmark motion tracking for swipesPoses and movements need different methods; both stay fast on the edge and cope with glare
    DeploymentAI core and API middleware both on one Nvidia Jetson Orin Nano 8GBA single edge device; low latency; keeps working if the network drops
    PrivacyLandmarks only, no video storedSimpler sign-off from legal and customers
    IntegrationThe client's existing ordering system, via its APINo replacement needed; gesture orders flow into the system staff already use
    Design challenges

    What the architecture had to solve

    Lighting and glare

    Sun on windscreens, night shifts, headlights.

    → A high-resolution camera with infrared and tuned exposure

    Whose hand is it?

    Passengers and kids in the back also wave.

    → A driver-side region of interest

    Accidental gestures

    A stray movement must never place an order.

    → On-screen feedback and a confirm step

    First-time customers

    People don't expect to order with their hands.

    → An on-screen gesture guide
